## 1. Brief Summary

Design and build a professional website for a **Canadian immigration practice** (law firm and/or Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant). The site must build trust fast (visitors are often anxious, time-pressed, and comparing several firms), be easy to navigate for non-native English/French speakers, and convert visitors into consultation bookings.

---

## 2. Goals

1. Establish credibility and trust within seconds of landing (credentials, regulatory body membership, track record, clear specialization).
2. Make it effortless to find the right service for the visitor's specific situation (Express Entry, PNP, sponsorship, study/work permit, citizenship, refugee claim, etc.).
3. Drive one primary action: **book a consultation** (call, form, or scheduling link).
4. Be usable and readable for non-native English/French speakers and under stress — short sentences, no legal jargon walls.
5. Rank for local + practice-area search terms across Canada (SEO), bilingual where relevant.

---

## 3. Target Audience

- Prospective immigrants navigating Express Entry, Provincial Nominee Programs (PNP), family sponsorship, study/work permits, citizenship
- Employers needing LMIA-based work permits or intra-company transfers
- Refugee claimants and people navigating the Immigration and Refugee Board (IRB) process
- People facing inadmissibility, removal orders, or appeals — often urgent, high-stress
- Often first contact is mobile, often not in English or French as a first language

## 5. Key Content Requirements

- **Credentials section** must state clearly whether the practitioner is a **lawyer** (Law Society of [Province]) or an **RCIC** (licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, CICC) — this distinction matters to clients and is often a trust/compliance requirement.
- **Plain-language explanations** of each immigration pathway before any legal detail — assume the reader has never applied before.
- **Clear, repeated CTA**: "Book a Free Consultation" (or firm's actual offer) on every page, not just the homepage.
- **Testimonials/results**, worded within the advertising restrictions of the relevant regulator (see §7) — avoid guaranteed-outcome language.
- **FAQ section per pathway** — reduces anxiety and pre-qualifies leads before they call.
- **Urgency path for detention/removal/appeal deadlines** — a visibly different, faster CTA ("Facing removal or a deadline? Call now") since IRB and Federal Court timelines are often strict.

---

## 6. Design & Tone

- Warm but authoritative — not corporate-cold, not casual. Photos of real staff, not generic stock imagery (flags, maple leaves, gavels).
- High contrast, large readable type — audience skews toward stress-reading, varying digital literacy, and translation via browser tools.
- Avoid overused Canadiana/legal clichés; favor human, approachable imagery (families, newcomers, office/team photos).
- Mobile-first: assume most visitors land from a phone search during a stressful moment, sometimes from outside Canada.

---

## 7. Compliance / Regulatory Requirements (confirm specifics with the firm before publishing)

- **Identify the correct regulator**: Law Society of the applicable province/territory (for lawyers) or the **College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC)** (for RCICs) — licence/membership number should be displayed, typically in the footer and About page.
- Advertising rules vary by regulator and province — no guaranteed-outcome language, required disclaimers on testimonials, and rules on how "specialist" claims can be worded.
- **Bilingual requirement**: if the firm serves Quebec clients or federal government contracts are involved, French-language content may be required, not just offered — confirm with the firm.
- **PIPEDA compliance** for any contact form or data collection (Canada's federal private-sector privacy law); include a privacy policy covering how client information submitted via the site is stored and used.
- Accessibility: follow **WCAG 2.1 AA**; note that **AODA** (Accessibility for Ontarians with Disabilities Act) applies if the firm operates in Ontario and meets AODA thresholds.
- Clear disclaimer that no lawyer/RCIC-client relationship is formed via the website or contact form until a retainer/engagement is signed.

## 8. SEO / Local Search

- Target pathway + location keywords ("Express Entry lawyer Toronto", "immigration consultant near me", "spousal sponsorship lawyer Vancouver", "[city] refugee lawyer")
- Google Business Profile integration, embedded map, NAP (name/address/phone) consistency per office location
- FAQ schema markup for common questions (processing times, eligibility, PNP draw scores)
- Blog/resources section to capture long-tail queries tied to IRCC policy changes, PNP draw results, and processing time updates — these change often and drive recurring search traffic

---

## 9. Technical Notes

- Fast load on mobile/low bandwidth (visitors may be applying from abroad or on limited data plans)
- Contact form must have a clear confirmation + fallback phone number (don't make urgent detention/appeal cases wait on email)
- Scheduling integration (Calendly or similar) if the firm offers direct consultation booking
- Language toggle (EN/FR) built properly (separate URLs/hreflang tags), not a browser auto-translate band-aid, if bilingual content is required
- Analytics + call tracking so the firm can see which pages drive actual consultations, not just traffic
